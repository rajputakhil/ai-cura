Title: Functional characterization of a KCNQ1 missense variant and phenotype specificity in
Long QT syndrome type 1
Authors: K. Fictional, L. Example (Fictional Cardiac Electrophysiology Consortium)
Recruitment: 5 unrelated probands with a clinical diagnosis of Long QT syndrome (LQTS), identified
through a cardiology genetics clinic, each undergoing a comprehensive cardiac genetic workup.

Target variant: NM_000218.3:c.1030C>T (p.Arg344Cys)

FUNCTIONAL STUDIES
The variant was introduced into a heterologous expression system (CHO cells) and characterized by
whole-cell patch-clamp electrophysiology. Compared with wild-type IKs channel current and a
well-characterized non-functional (null) channel construct used as the abnormal control, channels
containing the p.Arg344Cys subunit showed markedly reduced current density, consistent with a
loss-of-function effect on the slow delayed rectifier potassium current. Both the wild-type and
null controls were included in every recording session, and these experiments were performed by
the study authors.

PHENOTYPE
All 5 carriers presented with a corrected QT interval (QTc) greater than 480 ms and a clinical
history of exercise-induced syncope or aborted cardiac arrest, the phenotype classically associated
with LQT1. Comprehensive cardiac genetic testing (a validated arrhythmia gene panel) excluded
pathogenic variants in other Long QT syndrome genes in all 5 carriers, supporting that this
phenotype is highly specific to KCNQ1-related disease in this cohort.

No case-control cohort comparison was performed for this variant. No family members were available
for segregation testing in any of the 5 families. No RNA-based splicing study was performed. No
trio testing was performed, so de novo status could not be assessed.
