Title: Cosegregation of an LMNA missense variant with autosomal dominant dilated cardiomyopathy
in a five-generation family
Authors: C. Example, D. Fictional (Northern Demonstration Cardiogenetics Group)
Recruitment: a single five-generation family with a history of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and
conduction disease was ascertained through a proband referred for cardiac genetic testing.

Target variant: NM_170707.4:c.1130G>A (p.Arg377His)

SEGREGATION
The variant was tracked by Sanger sequencing across all available family members. It cosegregated
with clinically confirmed DCM and/or conduction disease in 8 affected relatives across three
generations, in addition to the proband. Two individuals were obligate carriers based on their
position in the pedigree (each has an affected parent and an affected child) and were counted as
carrying the variant without direct genetic confirmation. No genetically confirmed carrier over
age 40 was free of cardiac disease. This is an autosomal dominant pedigree with no consanguinity
and no history suggestive of recessive inheritance.

No case-control cohort was assembled for this variant, so no enrichment statistic is reported. No
functional studies of the variant were performed. No RNA-based splicing study was performed. The
proband's phenotype (DCM with conduction disease) is common to many cardiomyopathy genes and is
not considered specific enough to LMNA on its own to support a phenotype-specificity claim.
