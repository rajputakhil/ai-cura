Title: A recurrent MYBPC3 missense variant in familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy:
functional and segregation evidence
Authors: R. Fictional, S. Example, T. Placeholder (Institute of Demonstration Cardiology)
Recruitment: 41 unrelated probands with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) were prospectively
recruited at a single tertiary center between January 2018 and December 2022. All probands met
established HCM diagnostic criteria (maximal LV wall thickness >= 15 mm unexplained by loading
conditions). Whole-exome sequencing was performed on all probands.

Target variant: NM_000256.3:c.1504C>T (p.Arg502Trp)

CASE ENRICHMENT
The variant was identified in 6 unrelated probands in our HCM cohort (6/41). It was absent from
2,504 in-house control exomes from individuals without cardiac disease. Each of the 6 carriers was
an independent proband; only the proband was counted per family. The enrichment of this variant in
cases versus controls was statistically significant (p < 0.001).

SEGREGATION
In the three largest families, the variant was tracked by Sanger sequencing. Across these families
the variant cosegregated with clinically confirmed HCM in 7 affected relatives who were genetically
confirmed to carry the variant, in addition to the probands. Two obligate carriers were also
documented. No affected family member lacked the variant, and no genetically confirmed carrier over
age 50 was unaffected.

FUNCTIONAL STUDIES
We expressed wild-type and p.Arg502Trp cardiac myosin-binding protein C in a neonatal rat
cardiomyocyte model. Compared with the wild-type control and a well-characterized null (truncating)
construct as the abnormal control, the p.Arg502Trp protein showed markedly reduced sarcomere
incorporation and accelerated proteasomal degradation, consistent with a loss-of-function effect.
Both normal (wild-type) and abnormal (null) controls were included in every experiment. These
functional assays were performed by the study authors.

PHENOTYPE
All six carriers displayed the characteristic HCM phenotype (asymmetric septal hypertrophy, myocyte
disarray on available histology). HCM in the setting of an MYBPC3 variant is highly specific to
sarcomeric cardiomyopathy, and the genetic workup (WES) excluded other sarcomeric-gene variants in
all carriers.

No RNA-based splicing study was performed for this variant.
